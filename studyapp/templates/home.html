Built‑in Academic Algorithms

These aren't just features — they're the three algorithms this project implements from scratch. Here's exactly what each one does, and why it actually helps you study.

🧠 TextRank — Note Summarization

How it works: Your note is split into sentences. Each sentence is compared to every other sentence by how many words they share, forming a graph of connections. A PageRank‑style scoring pass then runs over that graph — sentences strongly connected to many other important sentences rise to the top, the same way an important webpage is one that many other important pages link to.

Why it helps you study: Instead of re‑reading a full page of lecture notes the night before an exam, you get the 2–3 sentences that actually carry the core ideas — a fast way to refresh your memory across many topics without losing the key facts.

📐 TF‑IDF — Note Search

How it works: For every word in your search, the system measures how often it appears in one specific note (term frequency) against how rare that word is across all your notes (inverse document frequency). Common words like "the" or "study" score low everywhere; specific terms like "mitochondria" or "recursion" score high wherever they actually matter.

Why it helps you study: Search across hundreds of saved notes and get the one that's actually about your topic first — not just any note that happens to mention the word once in passing.

⚡ Priority Scheduler — Task Ordering

How it works: Each task gets a weight for how important it is (High/Medium/Low) and a separate urgency score that climbs the closer — or further past — its deadline gets. The two are multiplied together and tasks are sorted by that combined score.

Why it helps you study: Your to‑do list reorders itself automatically as deadlines approach, so a high‑priority assignment due tomorrow always surfaces above a low‑priority one due next month — even if you added them in the wrong order.
